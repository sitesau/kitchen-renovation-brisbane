Where your budget goes

Labour typically represents 40 to 50 per cent of total cost. Cabinetry, benchtops and appliances follow, with flooring, electrics, plumbing and finishes rounding out the bill. In Brisbane, two factors tend to lift costs: first, adapting older Queenslander homes where plumbing and electrical runs are less straightforward; second, the trend toward open-plan living which often requires structural consultation. Premium materials such as engineered stone, custom joinery and integrated appliances push budgets toward the higher end.

Building permits and regulations for Brisbane kitchens

Queensland regulates domestic building work carefully. Understanding the rules protects both your home and your investment.

  • QBCC licensing. Any kitchen work valued over $3,300 must be carried out by a QBCC-licensed building contractor. Verify the licence on the QBCC website before you sign.
  • Domestic Building Insurance. Projects over $3,300 must be covered by Domestic Building Insurance under the Queensland Building Sector Reforms. This protects you if the builder cannot complete or fix defects.
  • Building permits. Permits are required for structural changes, new plumbing, electrical work, or changes to load-bearing walls. Simple cosmetic updates, such as new cabinetry in the existing layout without moving services, generally do not need a permit.
  • Electrical and plumbing trades. Electrical work must be done by a licensed electrician. Plumbing work (including new or relocated services) must be carried out by a licensed plumber. Both must be current and authorised for domestic work.
  • Council requirements. Brisbane City Council may have additional advice on water and waste service relocations; check their website if your project affects those lines.

Design trends and practical considerations for Brisbane kitchens

Brisbane's market trends toward open-plan layouts that blur the kitchen into living and dining zones. Cabinetry tends toward light oak, pale grey or white finishes, with waterfall island benches (where the benchtop extends vertically down one side of the island) now the standard for mid-to-premium renovations. Appliances favour integrated built-ins and induction cooktops. Ventilation matters: tropical humidity and frequent cooking mean a proper rangehood or integrated extraction is essential.

Queenslander homes present specific challenges: original kitchens were often cramped; high ceilings and verandahs mean unexpected structural considerations; and older plumbing may run through timber framing rather than accessible walls. A kitchen renovation in a Queenslander often costs more upfront but rewards with a home that feels cohesive in style.

The kitchen renovation process: what to expect

A well-managed kitchen renovation follows a logical sequence from scope to hand-over. Knowing the steps helps you spot a thorough builder from a thin one.

  1. Design consultation. Discuss layout, cabinetry style, benchtop and appliance choices, and confirm your budget band. Identify any structural or service-relocation work upfront.
  2. Formal quote and permit application. Obtain a fixed-price quote and, if needed, arrange building and electrical permits with the builder's support.
  3. Demolition and rough-in. Strip the old kitchen, complete rough electrical and plumbing (if required), then frame and prepare surfaces.
  4. Cabinetry installation. Install base and wall cabinetry, ensuring levels and alignment. This is often the longest single phase.
  5. Benchtops and splashback. Fit benchtops (stone, composite or laminate), seal as needed, and install the splashback tiling or cladding.
  6. Finishes. Install appliances, lighting, flooring (if included), drawer pulls and hardware, then final sealing and clean-up.
  7. Inspection and handover. Final walk-through, defect check, and sign-off once all work is complete.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a permit for a kitchen renovation in Brisbane?

Yes, if the work involves structural changes, new or relocated plumbing, electrical modifications, or moving load-bearing walls. A simple benchtop swap or cabinet refresh in the same location typically does not need a permit. Work over $3,300 must be done by a QBCC-licensed builder.

How long does a kitchen renovation typically take?

A straightforward mid-range kitchen usually takes 6 to 8 weeks once work starts. Custom kitchens, plumbing relocations, and structural changes can extend this to 10 to 12 weeks or longer. Always allow extra time for custom cabinetry delivery.

What is the most expensive part of a kitchen renovation?

Labour is typically the largest cost at 40 to 50 per cent of the total, followed by cabinetry and benchtops. Premium appliances, structural work and custom joinery add significant cost to mid-to-luxury renovations.

Can I renovate a kitchen in a Queenslander?

Yes, and many Brisbane kitchens are in character homes. Queenslanders often require careful planning because plumbing and electrics may run through timber framing, and structural changes need engineer sign-off. A builder experienced in period homes is essential.
